01 · Project overview

From messy source notes to decision-ready data

This project answers one question: if a client hands me inconsistent information and asks me to organize, verify, analyze, and summarize it — can I deliver something useful? The full workflow below demonstrates that the answer is yes.

The scenario

A fictional small-business consultancy, Riverstone Advisory, needs a structured view of the AI productivity tools market so it can brief clients on how vendors position themselves, what they charge, and who they serve. All companies, prices, and sources are simulated — the workflow is what's real.

Why fictional data? A portfolio should never present unverified claims about real companies. Using reserved .example domains and invented vendors keeps every number in this project auditable against its own dataset — which is exactly the discipline real research requires.

What this demonstrates

  • Research criteria defined before gathering data
  • Messy, inconsistent input transformed into a structured table
  • Uncertainty flagged with a visible confidence system — not hidden
  • Analysis computed from the dataset, never invented
  • Concise, decision-oriented business reporting

Relevant for: web research · data entry & cleaning · market research · AI-assisted research · business reporting.

03 · Research methodology

A repeatable eight-step workflow

The same process would run on a live engagement — only the sources change.

  1. 1

    Define research criteria

    Fix the field list, category definitions, pricing normalization rules, and confidence levels before collecting anything.

  2. 2

    Gather source information

    Collect product websites, pricing pages, directories, and review pages — each record tagged with source type and date.

  3. 3

    Extract relevant facts

    Pull only the fields that matter from each source into working notes, preserving the original wording.

  4. 4

    Normalize fields

    Standardize currency, billing cycles, dates, yes/no values, capitalization, and category labels.

  5. 5

    Remove duplicates

    Match on name + website, keep the strongest record, and log every merge.

  6. 6

    Flag uncertainty

    Mark single-source, derived, vague, and conflicting values with a confidence level.

  7. 7

    Verify critical claims

    Re-check pricing and availability against the most authoritative source; resolve or flag conflicts.

  8. 8

    Summarize findings

    Compute patterns from the cleaned data and write the decision-oriented summary.

Where AI assists

  • Extracting fields from source text
  • Summarizing long product pages
  • Categorizing products against the agreed taxonomy
  • Normalizing terminology and formats
  • Identifying missing or inconsistent information
  • Drafting summaries for human editing

AI accelerates the repetitive 70% — it never signs off on the result.

Where human review is required

  • Verifying facts against sources
  • Checking pricing and billing details
  • Resolving ambiguous or conflicting data
  • Confirming duplicates before merging
  • Confirming source credibility
  • Making final judgments and sign-off

Every "Needs verification" flag on this page is a decision only a human should make.

08 · Competitive summary

Key findings

All percentages are calculated directly from the cleaned dataset — none are estimated.

70%

Pricing clusters in the middle band

14 of 20 products charge $6–$14.99 / month (median $9.50). Automation ($19.99) and meeting intelligence ($15.00) price highest; simple kanban and note-taking price lowest ($4.99–$5.50).

70%

The buyer is a small one

8 of 20 target individuals & freelancers, 6 target small teams / SMB. Only 2 claim an individual-to-enterprise range and none is enterprise-only — mid-market is the ceiling for most of this sample.

85%

AI is table stakes — but unevenly explained

17 of 20 advertise AI features. Descriptions range from concrete ("daily plan generator") to vague ("AI assistant — details unclear"), which makes real feature depth hard to compare from marketing pages alone.

3 plays

Differentiation follows three strategies

Bundling (all-in-one workspaces), workflow depth (automation, AI forecasting), and AI-assisted planning / summarization. Price is the most crowded lever; only 1 of 20 competes on flat non-per-seat pricing.

65%

Free plans are the norm — tightly capped

13 of 20 offer a free tier, typically limited by usage caps (5 meetings/mo, daily caps, limited automation runs, 1 free project) rather than removed features. Only 2 products have no free access at all.

Gaps

Where the sample is underserved

No enterprise-grade option and no mobile-first product appear (2 of 20 lack a mobile app entirely). Transparent AI usage limits are rare, and annual-billing terms are stated for only 1 of 20 products.

09 · Research quality control

Reliability, not just speed

The checklist applied to every row before this deliverable was considered finished.

✓

Source recorded

Each cleaned row maps to its raw record (R01–R22), each with a source type and last-checked date; derived or uncertain values are documented.

✓

Duplicate checked

Two duplicate listings (R21, R22) matched on name + website and merged with a log entry.

✓

Pricing normalized

All prices normalized to USD per month (per-user where stated); annual-only converted ($108/yr → $9.00/mo) and documented.

✓

Missing values flagged

Unknowns are marked in Notes — never guessed, never silently dropped.

✓

Unclear claims marked for verification

WorkWhale (price conflict) and GoalGrid (unclear platforms) carry "Needs verification".

✓

No unsupported assumptions

Values the sources don't state were removed in a field-by-field traceability audit; remaining derivations (FocusForge monthly assumption, ChronoBase conversion) are documented in Notes.

✓

Dates standardized

All last-checked dates converted to ISO 8601 (YYYY-MM-DD) for sorting and freshness checks.

✓

Final review completed

Dataset consistency was reviewed during project QA — every cleaned row re-audited field-by-field against its raw record.

The verification stance

Speed is only useful if the output can be trusted. Where sources disagreed or were unclear, the conflict or gap is still visible in the data. Where a value was derived or assumed, the derivation is written next to it. Each cleaned row maps back to a raw record ID — that is the standard this workflow holds itself to.

AI drafts; a person verifies. Nothing in the final dataset is presented as verified unless a human checked it.

11 · Final client summary

Executive summary

The one-page version a client would actually receive.

Objective

Map how 20 AI productivity tools (fictional demo vendors) position themselves — features, pricing, and target customers — to support advisory conversations with small-business clients.

Key findings

  • Pricing clusters at $6–$14.99 per user / month (14 of 20 products; median $9.50).
  • 70% of products target individuals & freelancers or small teams; no enterprise-only option appears.
  • 85% advertise AI features, but depth is often vague or unquantified.
  • 65% offer a free plan, usually with tight usage caps.

Notable patterns

  • 17 of 20 state per-seat pricing; 2 don't specify the pricing basis; 1 is flat (SummarEase).
  • Annual-billing terms are stated for only 1 of 20 (InBoxZero); 1 product is annual-billing-only (ChronoBase).
  • Free-tier limits favor usage caps over feature removals — a low-friction trial path.
  • All-in-one workspaces bundle broadly, while niche tools win on workflow depth.

Potential opportunities

  • Transparent AI usage limits + flat pricing — only 1 of 20 competes there.
  • Mobile-first AI assistance — 2 of 20 have no mobile app and none is mobile-first.
  • Enterprise-ready task management with a clear security / compliance story.
  • Clearer annual-pricing disclosure as a low-cost trust differentiator.

Items requiring further verification

  • WorkWhale pricing — $14 (official page) vs $12 (directory); conflict unresolved.
  • GoalGrid mobile platform availability — source list unclear.
  • MindMesh pricing — single source only.
  • Annual-discount terms: only InBoxZero states one; the rate is unpublished.
  • Specific AI model / usage limits behind vague "AI assistant" claims.
